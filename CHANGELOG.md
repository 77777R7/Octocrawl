## Unreleased

- **A finished job on your computer leaves each page's Markdown as a file of its own.** A server on your computer wrote `results.jsonl` and `results.csv` when a crawl or batch ended, so an agent asked for the pages as Markdown files wrote a script to split the JSONL, and a coding agent stops to ask before running one. It now also writes `markdown/0001-<host-path>.md`, a file a page with content (`success` or `partial`; a failed, blocked or empty page has none), numbered as the results are; `results.csv` gains `markdown_file`, as `--out` writes it, the status names the directory in `resultFiles.markdown`, and `get_batch`, `wait_batch`, `get_crawl` and `wait_crawl` say to hand those files on.
- **A batch says when it read pages and took nothing from them.** In a new-user run on 0.8.7, a batch asking `json` for each GitHub repository's `license` came back `complete` with `null` on 20 of 20 pages and no hint, and a batch with an `includeTags` that matched nothing came back with empty Markdown on all 20; the agent read 100 pages in five batches to find a way in. A `json` field left `null` for want of a source now carries a hint on its page: `json` fills a field from data the page states for it or from a label matching the field's name, so name the field as the page labels it, read the value from the Markdown, or name its element in an `attributes` or `list` format; and a model reads a field only when the schema does not allow it `null`. A finished batch on your computer says how many pages read held nothing selected, and which `json` fields had no source on how many.
- **A compact `scrape` of a page its site adapter could not vouch for returns its Markdown too.** Without `formats`, a compact scrape (the MCP `scrape`, `octocrawl scrape`, an SDK `scrape` with `debug: false`) of a page on a site with an adapter (X, Amazon) was answered with the adapter's JSON alone, even when the adapter's check of the page failed (`adapterValidation.valid: false`): an X home timeline read in your Chrome came back `success` and `ready` with an empty incomplete JSON and no Markdown, and the agent had to ask again. Such an answer now carries the page's Markdown beside the JSON.

## 0.8.8 — 2026-11-07

The published packages at 0.8.8. **A sign-in in your Chrome no longer holds your agent:** a my-browser read at a sign-in nobody acts on for 90 s ends `blocked` with `login_wall` and leaves the tab open, so your agent can tell you what the page needs. The page that asks you to allow sites shows its text in a dark Chrome and says how many sites it asks for. A finished batch says what to do about every failed page, a line a reason. A page read in a tab out of sight keeps what it shows.

- **A finished batch's line about one failed page reads as one page.** The lines a finished batch's status carries about its failed pages said "1 page of example.com are disallowed by their robots.txt", "1 page … gate automated access" and "ran out of their timeout"; each now agrees with its count.

- **A page read in a tab out of sight keeps what it shows when the whole document misleads.** The `my-browser` lane reads a page whose tab stays behind another window from its whole document, CSS-hidden parts included (`page_read_hidden`). An exhibitor page of www.english.ids-cologne.de hides its product groups in a folded accordion; read whole, the extractor took them for a list at confidence 0 and dropped the company's name, address and contacts, and the page still answered `success` and `ready` (1 of 272 pages on 2026-11-07, read while the person had switched tabs). The lane now also reads the page as it shows in that tab, and takes the content from it when the whole document's main content is a weak guess (confidence under 0.5, or none found) and the page as it shows gives a surer one. The raw HTML stays the whole document; a sure whole document, `includeTags`, `onlyMainContent: false` and a list's `itemSelector` keep today's reading. The `page_read_hidden` message says which was read, and the trace's `user_browser_read` says `readAs`.
- **A finished batch says what to do about every failed page, a line a reason.** Its status (`GET /v1/batches/:id`, the MCP `get_batch` and `wait_batch`) carried a line for the pages that asked for a login and nothing for the rest: an agent read 28 failed items of a 124-URL batch to learn that 11 only needed the browser lane `fastMode` had declined, and 11 more had a robots.txt hosted Octocrawl could not reach. The status now groups the failed pages by what stopped them, most pages first, each with the step that reads them: a new batch without `fastMode`; what this server obeys for robots.txt and a challenge, and the route on your own computer; a longer `timeout` up to the server's ceiling; and one line for every other reason that points at `get_batch_errors`. Hosted jobs have the same lines, from counts the worker keeps as the job runs (`failures` on the job), so a 1,000-URL batch is summed without reading its items back; a job started before this change has none. Each item keeps its own hint.
- **A my-browser read of a site you are not signed in to no longer holds your agent for 10 minutes.** If the page asks you to sign in and nobody acts on it within 90 s, the scrape ends `blocked` with `login_wall`, the tab stays open in your Chrome, and your agent can tell you what the page needs. Once you click or type on the page it waits the full 10 minutes as before. A `handoff.waitMs` you give is kept, and batches are unchanged. The page that asks you to allow sites now says a sign-in waits for you.
- **The page that asks you to allow sites shows its text in a dark Chrome.** The page Octocrawl opens in your Chrome before it reads there set a dark text colour and no background, so a dark Chrome showed two buttons on black, with the task and the sites invisible. It now has its own background, looks like octocrawl.dev, with Octocrawl and Chrome joined at the top and a glyph sea rolling below, and says how many sites it is asking for. What it asks, and what its buttons do, did not change.

## 0.8.7 — 2026-11-07

The published packages at 0.8.7. **A page behind a login now says what to do:** the hint and the MCP instructions tell an agent to ask the person first, naming the site, and give the routes this server has, the person's signed-in Chrome among them. A page read with your login that shows what a reader without one sees carries `sign_in_prompt` and reads as `not_served`, where it was `success`, so a session that lapsed mid-run shows. A finished job says where every page is, a hosted job reads 8 pages at once and waits for credits instead of failing, and a batch waits out a site's limit on rate.

- **A finished batch says what to do about the pages that asked for a login, in one line.** `GET /v1/batches/:id` and the MCP `get_batch` and `wait_batch` tools now carry `agentHints` on a batch that has ended with pages at a `login_wall`: how many, on which sites (three named, then a count), and the routes this server has, so an agent asks the person once for the batch, not once a page. Each item keeps its own hint. Hosted jobs do not have it yet: their items do.
- **A page waiting its turn for a browser slot keeps its whole timeout for its own read.** Where a server renders at most `browserPagesAtOnce` pages (hosted job slices: 2 of 8), a page queued behind slow renders no longer runs out of its `timeout` before it starts: its deadline stands still while it waits, never past the task's own, and the page's trace opens with `slot_wait` and the milliseconds waited (the ladder trace says `ladder_admission_waited`). On 2026-11-07 two of eleven browser pages of a hosted batch timed out at 30 s before any browser event.
- **A page behind a login says to ask the person, and names the person's own Chrome.** The hint of a `login_wall` named `mode authed` and an import command, and never the `my-browser` lane, which needs no import. It now tells the agent to ask the person first, naming the site, and gives the routes this server has for this request: their signed-in Chrome, a login saved once, or, on a server with neither, the person's own computer (it used to name `mode authed`, which such a server cannot serve). It names the site asked for, not a sign-in host the page redirected to. A saved login the site refused says so first, with the next step after it, and a page that still asks for a login in the person's own Chrome says to sign in there ([the hints](docs/scrape-options.md)). The text of these hints changed; hosted Octocrawl's did not.
- **An `includeTags` selection with nothing to read says so.** A selector that matches nothing, or only elements without text or an image (a JSON-LD script block, an empty element), still answers `success` with empty Markdown and `readiness` `ready`, and now carries an `empty_selection` warning and an agent hint naming what a selection leaves out and what reads it instead: `rawHtml`, or a selector of elements that hold text.
- **`octocrawl serve` and the command line's help say a page behind your login can be read in the Chrome you are signed in to.** On a server on this computer alone, `octocrawl serve` prints one more line when it starts: an agent is told to ask you first, then reads such a page in your Chrome or with a login you save once, and how many logins are saved. A server other machines reach and a hosted server print no such line. `octocrawl --help` names both routes, and `--lane my-browser` in `octocrawl scrape --help` and `octocrawl batch --help` says what it is for and that it needs remote debugging on.
- **A finished job says where every page is.** A server on your computer writes `results.jsonl` (a page a line, as `get_batch_items` lists them) and `results.csv` (a row a page, the CLI's evidence columns) beside the task's checkpoint when a crawl or batch ends, and the status names them in `resultFiles`; a hosted job's status carries `export`, a link that downloads every page as one gzip JSONL file, good for 24 hours, with a fresh link on each read. `get_batch`, `wait_batch`, `get_crawl` and `wait_crawl` add an `agentHints` line that says so, so an agent hands the person a file or a link instead of paging a thousand items through the chat. The CLI's `--out` writes the same columns, plus `markdown_file`.
- **A hosted job reads 8 pages at once, 2 of them in a browser, across up to 100 sites a slice.** A slice read 2 pages at once and took 20 hosts, so a batch of many light sites read about a page a host and 20 pages a slice; it also launched Chromium for every slice. Now its engine runs 8 workers, renders at most 2 pages at once (`browserPagesAtOnce`, a new engine option a local server may set too), takes up to 100 hosts with their leases asked for ten at a time, and the worker instance keeps one Chromium across its slices (`browserKeeper`). `maxConcurrency` on hosted goes up to 8. A day's pool with no room for a slice's headroom gives its pages alone, read with fewer workers, instead of pausing the job until the next day.
- **The MCP instructions say what to do at a login wall before an agent meets one.** A local server's instructions now say that a login wall Octocrawl recognises comes back `blocked` (`login_wall`), to ask the person first and name the site, to say when a page was read as them, and that a yes covers that site for the conversation. Hosted Octocrawl's say logins need a separate local server (`npx octocrawl serve`), not the hosted connection, and to ask first, naming the site. A read-only server's are unchanged.
- **A hosted job that runs out of credits waits instead of failing.** Its status stays `running` with `paused: { reason: "credits", limit, resumesAt, message }`, the message naming hello@octocrawl.dev for more credits; the job asks again every 6 hours and at the 1st, keeps what it read and spends nothing meanwhile (until now it failed with `credits_exhausted`). A scrape refused for credits names the same address. Operators can add bought credits once by an id (`CreditsStore.topUp`, documented in docs/hosted-api.md), and `W2L_TRIAL_CREDITS` adds a one-time pack the first time an account hits the wall (default off).
- **Hosted Octocrawl no longer names a path on its own server for a file.** A hosted read of a PDF, CSV or text file said it was `kept at /tmp/octocrawl-hosted/files/…`, in `agentHints`, `file.path` and the `artifacts` of `snapshot` and the Evidence Record, a place no client could read and that was swept after ten minutes. Hosted now saves no files: `file.path` is `null`, `artifacts` is empty, `file.sha256` still identifies the bytes, and a file with no Markdown names `npx octocrawl scrape <url>`, which saves it on your computer. A self-hosted `serve --hosted` server keeps its files as before.
- **A hosted refusal says which field in the answer and in the operator's log.** A hosted 400 for an option it does not take now carries `details.parameters`, as a local server's does. The `hosted_refused` log line covers every 4xx of the API routes, not only the gate's own, and a 400's line (and an MCP call's, when the tool refused its arguments) names the refused fields, never what was sent in them.
- **A batch walks back into a site's limit less often, and waits it out.** A page sent up to the home page that answers normally when read again is taken for a limit on rate: the wait in force, at most 8 s, stays the shortest for the rest of the run, where halving used to lead back to the same limit: www.english.ids-cologne.de hit it eight times in 272 pages. Two pages in a row sent up to the home page now leave that host alone for 60 s, so the pages read again come after the limit: a read inside it (about 45 s there) came back the same.
- **A refused map `limit` or `timeout` no longer names a local server's ceiling on hosted.** `limit: 0` on hosted answered "from 1 to 100000", while hosted takes at most 5000; a value that is not valid now says only what it must be, and one over the ceiling names this server's own.
- **A page read without your login says so.** A page read with your login (lane `my-browser`, a handoff, or mode `authed`) that shows a line like "Log in for contact details" now carries a `sign_in_prompt` warning, reads as `not_served` and counts in `notServed`, where it was `success` and `ready`: a session on www.english.ids-cologne.de lapsed for twelve minutes and 90 pages in a row lost their phone and email with no sign. The command line names how many.
- **A batch page that came nearly empty is read again, and says so if it stays that way.** A page of 300 characters of Markdown or fewer, under a quarter of the median of at least five other pages of its host in the batch, gets one more read after a wait; if it stays that short it carries a `thin_page` warning, and the command line names how many. Five exhibitors of www.english.ids-cologne.de came as a 213-character breadcrumb after a limit, each `success` and `ready` with no sign. A page that really is that short raises it too, so it stays `ready` and does not count in `notServed`, and it does not slow the host.
- **A refused `maxFileBytes` names this server's own cap.** `maxFileBytes: 0` on hosted answered "from 1 to 524288000", while hosted takes at most 5242880, and a value over hosted's cap named `W2L_MAX_FILE_BYTES`, a variable a hosted caller cannot set. A value that is not valid now says only what it must be, and one over the cap names this server's, with the variable only on a server it can change.

## 0.8.6 — 2026-11-07

The published packages at 0.8.6. **A batch reads a page again when the site pushed back on it:** a 429 or 503, a move up to the home page or a failed contract gets one more read after the host's wait, and only the last read is charged. It is on by default, so a batch a site limits takes longer; `retry: { softBlocks: 0 }` turns it off. A batch also counts and names the pages that are not what was asked for (`notServed`) and says its rate and how long it should take, the Evidence Record says what a saved login put into the browser, and a hosted job's day counts only the pages it read.

- **The Evidence Record says what a saved login put into the browser.** `access.sessionApplied` is `{ cookies, localStorageOrigins }`, counts alone, or `null` with no saved login, so a page read signed out can be told from a login that never arrived. It does not say the site took the login; a `verify` contract with `markdownExcludes` does ([Your own login](docs/access.md#your-own-login-mode-authed)).
- **A batch counts the pages read that are not what was asked for, and names them.** `GET /v1/batches/:id` and a crawl's status say `notServed`, the succeeded pages whose `readiness` is `not_served`, and the batch's errors report lists them under `notServed` with why (`redirected_off_page`, `excluded_text`, …); `errors` and `failed` stay the items that failed. Hosted jobs now report `redirectedOffPage` and `notServed` too, from the job's next start on.
- **A batch reads a page again when the site pushed back on it.** A page answered with a 429 or 503, sent up to the home page, or failing its contract is read once more after the host's wait (`retry: { softBlocks: N }`, default 1, up to 3, 0 off). Only the last read is kept and charged; `retried` on the status counts such pages, and each read set aside stays on the page's trace.
- **A host whose pages answer the same at any pace is no longer held back.** Slowed to 32 s between pages, with a page read again still answering the same, a host goes back to its own pace and the signal stops counting there (`settled` on the status). A 429 or 503 never settles.
- **Fix: a detail page a script replaced with the site's list passed as `success` and `ready`.** 39 of 60 exhibitor pages of www.english.ids-cologne.de came back as one 348-character list shell with no warning (2026-11-06). In a browser, a page whose script loads a path as deep with no segment in common now carries `redirected_off_page`.
- **Fix: a page that showed only a spinner image was read as that image.** An app whose first paint is one image with `alt="Loading..."` and no text passed the browser's loading check, so the answer was the spinner, `success` and `ready`, at the price of a browser page (teamziereis.de, 2026-11-06). Such an image on the first screen, on a page of at most 200 characters of text, now counts as a loading indicator: the page is waited for as a loading message is, up to 8 s. Beside more text, or below the first screen, the same alt stays a lazy image's placeholder.
- **Fix: a hosted job's day counted pages it never read, and a job stopped there said nothing.** A slice takes up to 101 pages from the day's job pools before it reads, reads 20 when every URL is on its own host, and kept the rest counted: on 2026-11-05 an account's 511 pages counted as 1,983 of 2,000, and a 295-URL batch stopped at 171 with its status still plain `running`. A slice's settle now gives back what it did not read, in the same commit as its credits, so the pools count pages read. A job a pool refuses says so on its status: `paused` carries the `reason`, the `limit`, `resumesAt` and a `message`, and `etaSeconds` is null until then.
- **Fix: the address check refuses every address no public site has.** It named loopback, private, link-local and metadata ranges and passed the rest, so a name could resolve to `240.0.0.0/4`, a documentation range or site-local IPv6 and be connected to, and an IPv4 address inside an IPv6 form (`64:ff9b::a9fe:a9fe`, `2002:a9fe:a9fe::`, `::169.254.169.254`) was not read at all. IPv6 outside global unicast is now refused, and the address an IPv6 form carries is judged as itself. `198.18.0.0/15` is refused on hosted and kept on a local server, where a proxy client's fake-IP mode answers DNS there. AWS's IPv6 metadata address had a wrong digit and is now named.
- **A batch says how long it should take, from what it knows.** A start answers `minimumSeconds`, the floor the per-host interval sets for reading every URL, and a running batch's status (`GET /v1/batches/:id`, `get_batch`, `wait_batch`) carries `rate`: `pagesPerMinute`, `etaSeconds` and `measuredPages`, measured on its own pages, so an agent knows when to come back. Both numbers are null until 5 pages have been read: no estimate is made from other sites. A local server measures on its latest 20 pages of the run, a hosted job on all of them since its first slice. `rate` is absent once the batch has ended.

## 0.8.5 — 2026-11-06

The published packages at 0.8.5. **A page that shows the wrong thing can now fail its contract:** `markdownExcludes` refuses a page that holds a given text, beside a check of what it must hold. The MCP instructions teach a directory as URLs first, then one `batch_scrape`; a hosted call with `access: "enhanced"` can read a site whose robots.txt the hosted network cannot reach; a `screenshot` is refused for what the caller lacks.

- **robots.txt read through `hosted_chrome`'s exit gets 12 seconds.** Every redirect hop opens a tunnel of its own, so two sites that do answer took about 6 and 8 seconds and timed out at 5. A direct lookup keeps its 5 seconds.
- **A hosted call that chose `access: "enhanced"` can read a site whose robots.txt the hosted network cannot reach.** A robots.txt that timed out or failed on the network used to stop the page at the first rung, and `octocrawl-chrome` read robots.txt outside its own exit, so it failed there too. The service now reads robots.txt through the exit its Chrome uses, behind the same address guard, and the ladder goes on to `hosted_chrome` for such a page. A rule or a 5xx still stops it, the rule found on that route is obeyed, and a call without `access: "enhanced"` is unchanged.
- **A task contract can say what a page must not show.** On 2026-11-04 a logged-out detail page, showing "Log in for contact details", came back `success`: every check could only ask for text, not refuse it. `markdownExcludes` (`text`) fails a page that holds the text, and `readiness` is then `not_served` with `excluded_text`. In a batch or crawl that failure also counts as a sign the site is pushing back, so such pages slow the host. It is taken only beside a check of what the page must hold: without one it is refused, because a page that never came, or the home page a site sent instead, holds no excluded text either. An MCP answer cut to its size budget is judged on the whole page, so a cut never clears it. The browser's wait for the region does not judge it, since text missing from a loading page proves nothing. The verifier is `verify/11`. The `verify` tool description no longer repeats the type names its schema lists.
- **The MCP instructions teach the directory route: URLs first, then one batch.** On 2026-11-04 a directory's 272 detail pages were read with one `scrape` call each. Where `batch_scrape` is listed, the instructions a client reads at `initialize` now say: get the detail pages' URLs first (`map` where a sitemap lists them, else `batch_scrape` the listing pages with `formats: ["links"]` and keep the detail links), then read them as one `batch_scrape`, not one scrape per page. To stay under the 2,000 characters a client keeps whole, four other sentences are shorter, and the rule said beside `browser_busy` reads "One scrape at a time in the browser per key or sign-in". `docs/batch-crawl-map.md` has the route in full (From a directory to its detail pages).
- **A hosted `screenshot` is refused for what the caller lacks, and a job's `screenshot` with `fastMode` at its start.** A caller without the browser lane (keyless, a key without `browser`, an account under 5 credits) that sent `formats: ['screenshot']` to `POST /v1/scrape` was told `screenshot requires the browser lane, which fastMode declines`, naming a `fastMode` the gate had pinned. It is still HTTP 400, now naming what the caller lacks and what to do instead, as a `waitFor` is. A hosted batch or crawl with `screenshot` and `fastMode` was accepted (HTTP 202), failed five slices in a row and ended `delivery_failed`, with nothing read or charged and no reason given. It is now HTTP 400 at the start (`screenshot requires the browser lane, which fastMode declines`), and a job already stored fails at its next slice as `request_refused`.

## 0.8.4 — 2026-11-05

The published packages at 0.8.4. **Every failure names its next step, locally and on hosted Octocrawl:** the command line says on stderr when a result is not the page and its help lists its own flags; `octocrawl serve` prints the command that connects Claude Code to it, and `npx -y @octocrawl/mcp` says at initialize when its API is not running; an `https://` URL of a site with no working HTTPS is read over `http://`, with an `https_unavailable` warning; a batch or crawl slows a host that pushes back and says so; `waitFor` with `fastMode` is refused by name. ## 0.8.1 — 2026-11-03

The published packages at 0.8.1. **Docs sites read better:** a page whose app fills its content after load (developer.apple.com/documentation) is waited for instead of read as its frame, Markdown that a site serves as `text/markdown` comes back as sent, and an MCP `scrape` answer cut at 50,000 characters can be read on with `markdownOffset`.

- **Fix: a documentation page whose app fetches its content after load is waited for, instead of being read as empty or as its menus.** developer.apple.com/documentation renders a still frame (a header, a footer, an empty app) and only fills its main content when its data request returns; with no loading indicator on the frame, the browser's settle ended after about a second and the frame was read as the page: `failed`/`empty_unverified` on a slow fetch, and, with the whole-page answer below, its hidden menus would have come back as `success`. When that page has more than 1,000 characters of Markdown, its wait was not cut short and it did not still show a loading indicator, it is now `success` with a `no_main_content` warning and a `whole_page_delivered` trace event, on the browser, provider and my-browser lanes. Pages read as a hidden, headings-only or framed region stay failed (#794).

## 0.8.0 — 2026-11-01

The published packages at 0.8.0, 227 commits after 0.7.0. **On your own machine the recommended route is now `installed_chrome` with `compatible_transport`, and Steel is no longer recommended;** nothing changes until you write the grant. A `markdownMatches` or `markdownCountMin` check now runs with a time limit, and one that does not finish fails its verification (`verification.undecided`) instead of holding the process. With an account, `crawl` and `batch_scrape` run as hosted jobs from any MCP client, the TypeScript SDK and the Python client, and the SDK's `watcher()` follows such a job by polling. Entries that name hosted Octocrawl describe its service, which is deployed apart from the packages.

- **Fix: the SDK's `watcher()`, `crawlAndWatch()` and `batchScrapeAndWatch()` poll a hosted job instead of ending with `unauthorized`.** Hosted Octocrawl serves a job's status and listings but not its `/events` and `/ws` routes, which it answers 403 `hosted_unavailable`. The watcher's default transport read every 403 on `/events` as a refused token and ended the watch with `error` `unauthorized` before it polled, so a hosted crawl or batch watched with the defaults never reported a page. It now moves on to polling on that refusal; any other 401 or 403 still ends the watch. `waitCrawl`, `waitBatch` and `transport: "poll"` were not affected.
- **Fix: a page no longer becomes `internal_error` when `hosted_chrome` fails; it keeps what the rungs before it read.** When the `octocrawl-chrome` service answered an error or could not be reached, its rung threw, and the throw ended the whole ladder run, and a job page (batch `813ed4ca…` on 2026-10-31) became `internal_error`, losing the page or the block the earlier rungs had found. The ladder now takes such a throw as that rung failing (`ladder_channel_threw` in the ladder trace): content an earlier rung read stays the answer, and otherwise the block the site gave an earlier rung does (`ladder_evidence_kept`). The read is still settled as one that threw, at its ceiling. Only `hosted_chrome` opts in; a paid provider's throw still ends the run with its paid calls on the error, and cancellation and the deadline keep their meaning.
- **Fix: a hosted instance no longer turns pattern checks off when its start-up self-test's backtrack-stack scan is slow.** That scan takes about 15 ms, but on 2026-10-31 a new `octocrawl-api` instance (`octocrawl-api-jobopts-011394e`) met the scan's 250 ms time limit first, at its start, and so refused `markdownMatches` and `markdownCountMin` for its whole life, while the same image passed at its build and on the job worker. A scan stopped at its time limit shows neither an engine error nor a crash, so the self-test now runs it again, up to 3 times, and fails only when every try meets the time limit. That revision never served traffic.
- **Fix: a hosted batch or crawl with `access: "enhanced"` reads through `hosted_chrome` again.** A hosted job's pages carry `skipTlsVerification: false`, and the hosted API's client sent it to the `octocrawl-chrome` service, which answers 400 to any option it does not read. Every job page that reached the rung then failed as `internal_error` (batch `813ed4ca…` on 2026-10-31, on `octocrawl-jobs-regex-4df8fee`). The client now sends a refused option only when it asks for something (`skipTlsVerification: true`, `mobile: true`, headers, actions, a screenshot), which the service still refuses by name. A scrape was not affected.
- **Hosted Octocrawl can take pattern checks, behind its operator's switch (ADR 0006, amendment of 2026-10-29).** With `W2L_HOSTED_REGEX_CHECKS=1` on the hosted API and the job worker, and a start-up self-test that passed, a scrape, crawl or batch may send `markdownMatches` and `markdownCountMin` checks, and the MCP schemas list them. Without the switch, or when the self-test fails, they are refused by name as before. The `octocrawl-chrome` service takes them when its own self-test passed, since its callers admitted them first.
  - **The self-test** checks that a scan stops near its limit, that a scan past the backtrack stack is an engine error, and that counts and matches equal `String.prototype.match`'s. The images' build runs it in the image just built, with every pattern of the repository's task files, and an image that fails it is not pushed.
  - **Node 22.23.4.** The `octocrawl-chrome` image moves from Node 26.8.1 to Node 22.23.4, the hosted API's, and both images pin it by digest: on Node 26.8.1 a scan stopped at its limit left every later match in the process about 3.7 times slower.

A hosted scrape the client cancels frees its browser lease and costs no credits (tested). (docs/mcp-client-ux-tasks.md W9)

(docs/mcp-client-ux-tasks.md W10)

- **JSON fields from what scholarly and news pages declare.** A JSON schema's `title` takes a page's `citation_title` before its first heading, so arXiv's abstract page no longer answers `Computer Science > Computation and Language` for its title. `description` takes `<meta name="description">`. Table rows labelled by a data cell ending in a colon (Wikipedia's taxobox `Kingdom:`, arXiv's `Cite as:`) are labels, and a line break in a value reads as a space. Wikipedia's cladograms (`table.clade`) are no longer given as data tables in `tables` or GFM tables in Markdown. ([docs/json-extraction.md](docs/json-extraction.md))

Its next tool call gets 401 at once, so the client asks to sign in again. A connection also ends after 90 days unused.

  - `server.json` lists `https://mcp.octocrawl.dev/mcp-oauth` as a second remote, so the MCP Registry shows it from this release.

A run that succeeds behaves as before.

The lane tells each page through `ExecutionContext.onListPage` and takes them back through `listResume`. A paginate step that fails at page N (a click that never lands) now keeps the records of the pages it read in `list`, with the failure in `actions.failed`.
- The Evidence Record's `access` block names the egress a page left through and the task session it was read with (ROADMAP PA item 3). `egress` is `{ proxy, source, switchedFrom }`. `proxy` is the proxy's `host:port`, never its credentials. `source` is `pool`, `environment` or `direct` on a request that got a page response. `switchedFrom` is the pool egress the task last moved off before the page was read, else `null`. `egress` is `null` when nothing says: a vendor's service, the person's own browser, or a lane that stopped before any page request. `session` is `{ id }`, never the cookies, `null` when none. Both are optional in the v1 schema file, so earlier records stay valid.
- The browser-compatible transport has a default host list (ROADMAP PA item 2). A local server whose access grant names `compatible_transport` and that sets no `W2L_COMPAT_HOSTS` uses it for five hosts. They are the hosts G1's two-window acceptance showed it helps (`research/access/benefit-hosts.v1.json`: fred.stlouisfed.org, www.idealo.de, www.investing.com, www.ironmountain.com, www.wayfair.com). `W2L_COMPAT_HOSTS=none` turns it off; naming hosts replaces the list. The list is a constant in the code, kept equal to the JSON record by a test, so every build has it.
- The README, the Chrome connection hint, the CLI prompt and the MCP `scrape` tool say that while Chrome's remote debugging is on, every page sees `navigator.webdriver` as `true`, Octocrawl connected or not. The handoff, `octocrawl login import` and the my-browser lane need remote debugging. This was seen on Chrome 153 with the chrome://inspect switch and on 154 with --remote-debugging-port. So a bot check may refuse the person's Chrome; turn it off when done.
- The my-browser lane waits 10 minutes, not 120 s, for the person to allow the sites in Chrome. A refusal says what Octocrawl's page last answered. A batch waiting for the approval reports `waitingForApproval: true`. The server logs each step of the approval (`my_browser_approval` on stderr). Three real-page runs had timed out at this step with nothing to tell a slow click from one not recognised.
- One plain choice of how pages are reached (ROADMAP PA item 7). On scrape and batch it is `"access": "standard"`, `"enhanced"` or `"my-browser"`. On crawl it is `standard` or `enhanced`. Standard uses no rung that costs a third party. Enhanced is what the server's access grant of tier enhanced approves, its providers in mode standard too. Without such a grant it is refused by name. My-browser is the person's own Chrome, as `lane: "my-browser"`. The choice is in the MCP `scrape`, `batch_scrape` and `crawl` tools and `--access` on the CLI. Batches and crawls keep the choice. The `/fc` shim maps Firecrawl's `proxy` onto it (`basic` is standard; `stealth` and `auto` are enhanced) instead of refusing it.
- The `my-browser` lane for batches (ROADMAP PA item 8): `"lane": "my-browser"` on `POST /v1/batches`, the MCP `batch_scrape` tool and `octocrawl batch --lane my-browser` read every page in the person's own Chrome, one at a time. The person allows every site of the batch (host and port) once for the run, in the page Octocrawl opens there; a page on another site, or after Revoke, is not read. Never cached. Refused with a webhook and with `maxConcurrency` above 1, besides what a scrape on the lane refuses.
- The `my-browser` lane (ROADMAP PA item 8), for one page: `"lane": "my-browser"` on `POST /v1/scrape`, the SDK, the MCP `scrape` tool and `octocrawl scrape --lane my-browser` reads the page in the person's own Chrome on a server on their machine. After Chrome's Allow, Octocrawl opens a page of its own there listing the site and the task. Only the person's click on **Allow reading these sites** lets it read that site without a further click. Closing that page or clicking Revoke stops it. Recorded as lane `my_browser` (a new value of `lane`), never cached. Refused by name on other servers and with actions, a screenshot, lockdown or a mode other than standard. Every Evidence Record's `access` gains `completion` (`unattended`, `authorized_session`, `user_browser`, `handed_to_person`, or null when no page was read). Batches and MCP `batch_scrape` follow.
- Managed sessions (`/v1/sessions/*`) no longer answer with the profile's path on the server (`profileDir`) or a CDP endpoint (`cdpEndpoint`); every route returns the public fields only. A hosted engine refuses them all (409), in the engine itself as well as at the hosted gate, and makes no browser profile (ROADMAP PA, G4).
