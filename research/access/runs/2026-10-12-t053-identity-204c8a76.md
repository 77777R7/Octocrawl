## Reading

- imf.org's edge (Akamai) refuses a headless Chromium that claims to be Google Chrome, on the first request, whatever the build, the full version, the brand set, the architecture, the platform or the proxy's level; it serves the same client when it says what it is (`HeadlessChrome`, stock or declared through the same CDP override). The product's standard identity ([compliance.ts](../../../packages/contracts/src/compliance.ts): the Chrome UA, `sec-ch-ua` with "Google Chrome", the `setUserAgentOverride` metadata) is what it refuses. How it tells is not isolated: the TLS and HTTP/2 fingerprint of a headless Chromium and the claimed brands disagree, and the first request carries no sensor data yet.
- This is ADR 0004's coherence rule from the other side: an identity the client does not have is not only a policy problem, here it is the block. On this site an honest headless identity is the route, and it costs nothing.
- It does not generalise by itself: on the 15 other tasks of G1's set, stock Playwright (the same honest identity) was refused too; Cloudflare and DataDome hold on other things. And a site that refuses "HeadlessChrome" by name would lose what the Chrome claim now gets. So the change is a measured one: an identity switch, run on the 92 tasks and the healthy controls in two windows, counted as verified tasks and false successes, before it becomes the default.

## What follows (for the task list's G2)

- T2.0 gains its first product-side candidate: a standard-mode identity that declares the real client (Chromium headless) instead of Google Chrome, behind a switch, measured A/B on item 1's set and the health batches. The declared identity is already what the Evidence Record names; the record would then say `HeadlessChrome`.
- T053 itself is expected to verify under that identity through the gateway (runs 3, 4 and 11); the control arm (C) through the gateway without it stays at 403.
