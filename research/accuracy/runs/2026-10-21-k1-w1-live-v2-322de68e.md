## How a live page is scored

`holdout_score.py` counts a field fully correct only when the answer's `rawSha256` equals the sealed capture's ("same source"). A live page never matches, so the scorer's own "required fully correct", "accepted" and false-success outcome would count every live answer as wrong.

The live rescoring therefore drops `sameSource` and keeps the scorer's other checks under its own rule: value and entity must hold; unit, date and period must hold where the gold states that context, and are not scored where the gold leaves it unknown (the scorer's `None`, as in its `fullyCorrect`). Five v2 prices (V08, V09, V11, V12, V14: no per-offer price time) and V24's population are such unknown contexts. Under a stricter rule that also fails unknown contexts, the totals would be Octocrawl json 66/294 with 1 accepted, Firecrawl json 113/294 with 9 accepted, Firecrawl markdown 103/294 with 8 accepted (reviewer's recount, not used in the tables). On that basis:
- **Accepted:** every required field is correct, with no duplicates and nothing fabricated.
- **False success:** the answer claimed success (status `success`, and for Octocrawl a verify that did not fail; Firecrawl and ZenRows are sent none) and was not accepted.

The gold is from 2026-10-20 captures. A value that changed since then (the first three advisories of a list, a rate, a forecast) is wrong for every arm alike. 60 of the 294 required captured fields are marked other than `static` in the gold, but list records marked `static` also move when new items come first. This drift is not isolated.

- **Firecrawl's LLM json delivers more correct required fields than Octocrawl's deterministic json, even with itemSelector:** 118 against 69 of 294. Its json fills article and document fields (title, authors, dates) that Octocrawl's deterministic json path leaves empty on these pages; on the frozen captures that path also had 29/294 without selectors.
- **Firecrawl also claims success on 36 of 42 cases, and 23 of those are not accepted.** Octocrawl's verify contract fails on almost every case, so Octocrawl claims success on 5–6. Octocrawl therefore makes far fewer false claims per request: 3–4 of 42 against 23 of 42. But it also delivers little that is usable under this json request.
- **The four Octocrawl arms score the same json fields.** v2 pages are mostly reachable over HTTP: 35–37 of 42 answered on the http lane. So the access route makes almost no difference here, and the candidate equals the control.
- **Live versus frozen, Octocrawl json with itemSelector on the same plan: 69 against 120 of 294.** The loss is in the lists whose first items changed since 10-20: V15–V19, V29, V32, V35, V26 and V31 in part. V10 and V13 failed live on all four local arms, and on Firecrawl. V21 failed on standard and enhanced; installed_chrome and control got a page that scored 0. V20 rose from 9 to 12. Firecrawl gets 0 on most of the same lists.
- **The same request on its Markdown** (Octocrawl's list plan returns no tables) scores 69/294, with 8 cases accepted. On the frozen captures, the Markdown request (with tables) gave 229/294. The two requests differ, so they are not compared here.
