## Deviations from the plan, stated

- The plan's sitemap bodies used Firecrawl's `scrapeOptions.includeLinks`; the native request takes `includeLinks` at the top level.
- B's include evidence is sitemaps.org's own: its homepage links `faq.php`, `protocol.php` and `terms.php` while its sitemap lists the `.html` pages, so every sitemap page is one the homepage does not link. D was not needed as the fallback the plan allowed for.
- C read gov.uk's first child sitemap whole: 5,569,718 bytes, under the 10 MiB wire cap, so the `unreadable` / `body_too_large` outcome the plan allowed for did not occur live; the bench test exercises it against a local server. The index has 35 children, not about 30.
- D's `pages_sitemap.xml` lists 133 URLs, not the 116 of the planning probe; the pages are its first five non-seed entries. The two `solutions/` pages were recorded in the other order, fetched by two workers at once.
- The sitemap reader and the http lane share one robots.txt cache per mode for the life of the API process: on the hosts whose sitemap was read (A, C, D) the reader made the one network read of robots.txt before the first page, which the API's output does not show as a timing (every page's `usage.timings.robotsMs` is below 0.1 ms there); B, D-skip and F's books.toscrape.com crawl were served robots.txt from that cache, as any later crawl of a host in one process always was. The one read per host is inferred from the shared cache, not observed in a log.
- A's `budgetExceeded` is null although 10 pages met `maxPages 10`: the load collected 10 entries, one of them the seed, so the frontier drained with nothing refused.
